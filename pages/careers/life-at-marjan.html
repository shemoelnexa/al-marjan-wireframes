Life at Marjan.

Stories from the team building the islands — first-person dispatches from the people behind the masterplan.

Six stories · One year · Forty-eight photographs

Al Marjan Island is a place under construction in every sense. The team behind it — 240 people across 31 nationalities — is building the masterplan in real time, learning as it scales, and writing the next chapter for Ras Al Khaimah while it does. These are six of their stories.

Edited by the Marjan People & Culture team · Photography by Ammar Al Attar

Spread 01 · From the Wynn site

"You can hear the concrete pouring from a kilometre away."

I joined Marjan in 2022, a few weeks before the Wynn announcement went public. I had spent eleven years on residential towers in Dubai Marina before that — different scale, different rhythm. Nothing prepares you for an integrated resort being lifted out of reclaimed land. The first time I stood at the perimeter fence and watched the foundation crew start the casino podium, I called my father in Sharjah. I did not know what to say to him. I just held the phone up to the site.

The day-to-day is unglamorous. I am on-site by 06:30, in the project office by 07:15, in coordination meetings until lunch. The afternoons are walks of the deck — listening to the rebar crew, the formwork foremen, the safety officers. It is the listening that matters. The plans tell you what the building should be. The site tells you what it is.

What surprised me most is that the master developer behaves like a builder, not a landlord. Marjan sits in the room with our delivery counterpart from Wynn Design & Development. We solve problems together. That is rare for the region.

By Ahmed Al Suwaidi · Senior Development Manager · with Marjan since 2022

Spread 02 · Inside the HQ

Marjan HQ, between meetings.

The headquarters sits on the central spine of Al Marjan Island, three storeys of cream limestone and bronze metalwork — finished in late 2024. There are 240 of us, growing to roughly 320 by end of 2027. The building was designed to feel less corporate than it is: open courtyards, a 60-cover canteen run by a Lebanese chef, a rooftop majlis facing the channel.

The kitchen tells you most about the culture. Lunch service runs from 12:30 to 14:15 — long enough for an actual conversation. The menu rotates Khaleeji, Levantine, South Asian, and Mediterranean weekly. On Wednesdays there is always machboos. On Thursdays, fattoush. On the last working day of the month, the chef hosts a tasting of whatever he has been experimenting with — currently a Bahraini-Filipino fusion that probably should not work, but does.

Spread 03 · A long way from Mayfair

"I moved here for a project. I stayed for a city."

I spent six years on the investor-relations desk at a London real-asset manager. Mostly UK build-to-rent, occasionally a continental hotel platform. When Marjan called me about the IR role in late 2023, I assumed it was a sovereign-backed sales pitch — slick pictures, no substance. I flew out for a site visit in February 2024 expecting to politely decline.

Two days in RAK reframed it for me. The work here is closer to a private-equity desk than a developer marketing function. I sit between Marjan's CFO, the sector leads, and the institutional investors — sovereign wealth funds from the Gulf, North American pension plans, European insurers. The product is a place; the conversations are about capital structures.

The trade-off is real. Friday brunches in Dubai are forty minutes by car. The cultural fabric of London is harder to replicate. But the work is harder to walk away from. I moved fully to RAK in September 2024 — apartment overlooking the channel, ten-minute commute to the office, twenty minutes to the beach. The maths is what it is.

By Sarah Chen · Investor Relations Director · with Marjan since 2024

Spread 04 · The sustainability programme

Coral, mangroves, and a corporate reporting deadline.

The sustainability team is twelve people now — three in 2022, doubling roughly every eighteen months. Half our time is in spreadsheets: GRESB submissions, the IFC Performance Standards gap analysis, the Scope 3 emissions baseline. The other half is in the water. We co-fund a reef-restoration programme with the Environment Protection & Development Authority of RAK, transplanting heat-resilient coral fragments onto reclaimed substrate around the islets. The 2025 cohort hit a 73% survival rate at the eighteen-month mark — higher than we modelled. The mangrove nursery at the northern islet is into its third growing season. None of this offsets a podium pour, but it is not nothing.

Spread 05 · Partnership work

"Brand standards are diplomacy, not paperwork."

I came to Marjan from Anantara, where I spent seven years on the asset-management desk across South-East Asia and the Maldives. My job here is to sit between the master developer and our hospitality operating partners — Hard Rock, Wynn, Anantara, Nikki Beach — and make sure the asset performs the way the brand promised and the way the developer underwrote.

It sounds clean on paper. In practice, brand standards are a moving target — operators evolve their handbooks every two or three years, and the development cycle is longer than that. By the time Hard Rock opens in 2028, their 2026 brand bible will have been refreshed twice. The work is keeping the design package, the construction reality, and the operator's evolving expectation in alignment without anyone losing patience.

The conversations I value most are the unscripted ones. Coffee in Hard Rock's Florida office. A walk-through with the Wynn pre-opening team. The brand wins or loses in those rooms — long before a guest ever walks through the lobby.

By Karim El-Masri · Hospitality Asset Manager · with Marjan since 2023

Spread 06 · A year in events

How the year is paced.

The internal calendar is its own social fabric. Six team events bookend each year — from the Ramadan iftar that brings every nationality to the same table, to the year-end dinner on the channel-side promenade.

February UAE Innovation Month · cross-team hackathon on the visitor-portal redesign
March Ramadan iftar · staff and partners on the rooftop majlis · 320 covers
June Family beach day · partners and children at the northern islet · early morning before the heat
September Graduate intake welcome · two-week immersion across sites and functions
November Sustainability week · field days on the reef and mangrove programmes · open to families
December Year-end dinner · promenade reception · five-year service awards announced
