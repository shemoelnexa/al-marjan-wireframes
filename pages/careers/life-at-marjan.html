An honest look at the people, the place and the pace of work at one of the region's most ambitious master developers.

Working on a place still being built

The island that Marjan is developing did not exist a generation ago — it was open water. That context shapes every meeting, every planning decision, and every conversation about what comes next in a way that is difficult to replicate in a market that is already fully formed.

The work is iterative in the truest sense. Decisions made in the planning team this quarter will be poured in concrete within eighteen months. There is no abstraction between the work and the outcome, and that directness is either energising or uncomfortable, depending on what you are used to.

Sara Hassan · Senior Development Manager

A small team, an outsized brief

Marjan carries the capital and infrastructure mandate for one of the Gulf's fastest-growing destinations with a permanent headcount that would fit comfortably into a single floor of a Dubai financial centre. That ratio is intentional — the organisation is designed to leverage partner expertise rather than internalise everything.

What it means in practice is that seniority at Marjan comes with real surface area. Investment professionals here sit across sovereign-wealth relationships, structured-finance decisions, and asset reviews that would be siloed across three separate functions at a larger house. The scope is not nominal.

Marwan Idris · Director of Investment

Living in Ras Al Khaimah

Ras Al Khaimah is a different proposition from Dubai — quieter, more coastal, with a slower social rhythm and a physical environment that rewards people who are interested in the outdoors. The Hajar Mountains are forty minutes from the office; the beach is ten. The cost of living is meaningfully lower, and the commute does not exist in the way it does in the southern emirate.

The international schools serving the Northern Emirates have expanded significantly over the last decade, and the RAK healthcare infrastructure has matured alongside the broader growth of the destination. The trade-off against Dubai's cultural density is real, but it is one that most Marjan employees make consciously and do not regret.

Aisha Al Mansoori · Sustainability Lead

Wellbeing, belonging, growth

The organisation runs structured learning, external mentorship, and a clear progression framework across every function — not as an aspiration but as a tracked commitment. More than a third of 2025 senior promotions came from internal moves across functions, which is the result of deliberate investment in the careers of the people already here.

The cultural fabric of the team is 40-plus nationalities in a single building, which creates a working environment that is genuinely international in its frame of reference. The People and Culture team structures the internal calendar to mark the significant moments across those communities — Eid, Diwali, national days, the year-end dinner on the channel promenade.
