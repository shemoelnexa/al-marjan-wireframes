01 The Thesis

A coastal city, being built.

Ras Al Khaimah's resident population is forecast to grow from 380,000 to 650,000 by 2030 — driven by hospitality jobs, family relocations from Dubai, and the long-term residency programme.

Knight Frank's RAK Residential Snapshot 2024 sizes the housing requirement against this growth and the current pipeline at a 45,000-unit shortfall — split roughly 60% apartment, 25% villa and townhouse, and 15% branded residence across the emirate.

Al Marjan Island captures a disproportionate share of that demand because it is the only master-planned coastal community at scale in RAK. Beachfront freehold, walkable urbanism, integrated retail, and proximity to the Wynn entertainment district lift both absorption and the price premium.

03 Product Types

Four residential asset classes.

From entry-level urban apartments to branded super-prime — the island delivers a spectrum that matches every common residential mandate, with consistent freehold title and a single development counterparty.

Apartments

Villas

4–7 bed beachfront and waterfront villas. From AED 9M, average AED 3,200 per sqft, net yields 5.0–6.0%. Owner-occupier and ultra-premium rental.

Branded residences

Wynn, Hard Rock, Nikki Beach, Anantara. From AED 4.5M, 25–35% price premium, operator-run rental pool, hotel-grade service inclusions.

Al Marjan Island has the deepest residential pipeline north of Dubai Marina — and the only coastal freehold product in the Northern Emirates with branded-operator participation at this scale. — Knight Frank RAK Residential Snapshot, 2024

05 Demand Drivers

Who buys, and why.

Demand is structurally diversified — 38% UAE residents trading up from Dubai, 22% GCC nationals seeking a second residence, 28% international Golden Visa applicants from Europe, India and Russia, and 12% institutional portfolios sized for rental hold. Average days-on-market for prime parcels has compressed from 142 days in 2022 to 47 days in Q3 2024.
