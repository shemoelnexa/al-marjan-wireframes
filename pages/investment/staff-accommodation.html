01 The Thesis

The yield play behind the cycle.

Every hotel key on the island generates 1.4–1.6 hospitality jobs. Every construction project generates 60–80 jobs per AED 100 million of CAPEX. The 6,000-key gap alone requires 8,400–9,600 hospitality staff at stabilisation.

Marjan's internal sizing — validated against EY's MENA Hospitality Workforce note 2024 — puts the staff accommodation requirement at 7,200 units by 2030, split 4,300 hospitality and 2,900 construction-cycle.

The product is uncorrelated to consumer cycles. Leases are signed by hotel operators and construction contractors against named covenants — not tenants. Average lease tenor is 10–12 years with annual UAE-CPI indexation, full repair and insurance terms, and bullet payments in advance.

03 Three Routes

Master-lease, build-to-suit, or operated.

Three structured routes — each with a different risk-return profile, depending on whether the investor wants pure covenant exposure, build-to-suit upside, or operating margin.

A·01 Master-lease
Route 01 · Lowest risk

Master-lease structure

Investor develops the asset, signs a single 10–12 year master-lease with a named operator — Wynn, Marriott, Hard Rock, Accor — who takes operating risk. Indexed rents, FRI terms, single covenant, no operating overhead. Target IRR 11–13%.

A·02 Build-to-suit
Route 02 · Balanced

Build-to-suit

Pre-committed build for a specific construction contractor or hospitality operator. Lease executed pre-construction, rent indexed, with cost-plus development margin. 10-year lease with two five-year extensions. Target IRR 12–14%.

A·03 Self-operated
Route 03 · Highest yield

Self-operated village

Investor owns and operates a multi-tenant workforce village — bed-night model, multiple operator and contractor tenants, in-house F&B and amenity. Higher operating margin, no single-tenant concentration. Target IRR 13–15%.

Workforce housing is the least glamorous and most reliably bankable real-estate trade on Al Marjan Island — a structural shortage met with named-covenant leases against the same operators behind the hospitality cycle. — Marjan investment thesis, 2024
