Anti-corruption policy

Marjan's zero-tolerance position on bribery, corruption, fraud, and conflicts of interest — and the channels available to report concerns confidentially.

Effective date 1 February 2026
Last reviewed 1 May 2026
Version v2.7
Jurisdiction Ras Al Khaimah, UAE
Article 01

Policy statement

Marjan adopts a zero-tolerance position on bribery, corruption, fraud, money laundering, and other dishonest conduct. The integrity of Al Marjan Island as a destination for investment, hospitality, and residency depends on the unimpeachable conduct of those who develop it, operate on it, and transact with it.

This policy sets out the standards that Marjan applies to itself and expects from every person who acts on its behalf or with whom it does business. It is approved by the Board of Marjan, reviewed annually by the Audit and Risk Committee, and given practical effect by the Group Compliance function.

The policy is to be read together with Marjan's Code of Conduct, its Gifts and Hospitality Standard, its Third-Party Due Diligence Procedure, and the confidential reporting channels described in Article 08. In the event of any inconsistency between this policy and any local procedure, this policy prevails.

Article 02

Scope

This policy applies, without exception, to all of the following:

  • Employees of Marjan and its subsidiaries, regardless of grade, location, or contract type.
  • Officers and directors of Marjan and of its subsidiaries.
  • Contractors and consultants engaged to perform work on behalf of Marjan, whether retained directly or through an agency.
  • Agents and intermediaries, including brokers, marketing representatives, and government-relations advisors.
  • Joint-venture partners where Marjan holds a controlling interest, and joint-venture partners more generally to the standards equivalent to those set out in this policy.
  • Vendors and suppliers providing goods, services, or works to Marjan or to projects under Marjan's master-development mandate.

Where a third party is unwilling or unable to apply standards equivalent to those in this policy, Marjan reserves the right to decline the relationship or to terminate it on notice.

Article 03

Standards and legal frameworks

This policy gives effect, in the operations of Marjan, to the legal frameworks listed below. Where two or more frameworks apply to a given matter, Marjan applies the stricter standard.

  • UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021 on the Issuance of the Crimes and Penalties Law, including its provisions on bribery of public officials and on the misuse of public funds.
  • UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 on the Criminalisation of Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism, as amended, together with the implementing regulations issued by the UAE Central Bank and the Executive Office for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism.
  • The OECD Anti-Bribery Convention and the related Good Practice Guidance on Internal Controls, Ethics and Compliance, applied as a matter of governance even where Marjan is not directly subject to the Convention.
  • The United States Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, where transactions or counterparties bring the activity within its jurisdictional reach.
  • The United Kingdom Bribery Act 2010, where transactions or counterparties bring the activity within its jurisdictional reach.
Article 04

Prohibited conduct

The following conduct is prohibited absolutely, whether committed directly or through a third party, whether the recipient is a public official or a private counterparty, and whether the conduct is intended to obtain an advantage for Marjan, for an individual, or for a third party.

  • Offering, promising, giving, requesting, agreeing to receive, or accepting any financial or other advantage with the intention of inducing or rewarding the improper performance of a function or activity.
  • Channelling payments, donations, sponsorships, or charitable contributions through any party in circumstances where the purpose or effect is to influence the decision of a public official or a counterparty.
  • Falsifying or assisting in the falsification of books, records, invoices, expense claims, or supporting documentation, whether to conceal a prohibited payment or for any other purpose.
  • Engaging in or facilitating fraud against Marjan, its partners, or any other party, including misappropriation, false accounting, and procurement fraud.
  • Failing to disclose an actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest in accordance with Marjan's Conflicts of Interest Standard.

A breach of this Article will be treated as a serious matter of discipline and may, depending on the facts, give rise to dismissal, contract termination, civil action, and referral to the competent UAE authorities.

Article 05

Gifts, hospitality and entertainment

Reasonable, occasional, and transparent gifts and hospitality may be exchanged in the ordinary course of business. Excessive, frequent, or undisclosed gifts and hospitality are not permitted. The following thresholds apply in addition to the principles set out in Article 04.

Article 06

Facilitation payments

A facilitation payment is a small, unofficial payment made to a public official to secure or expedite a routine action to which the payer is already entitled. Such payments are prohibited under UAE law and under this policy, irrespective of local custom, urgency, or any representation made by an intermediary.

Where a demand for a facilitation payment is made, the person to whom the demand is addressed must decline the payment, withdraw from the situation in a manner that preserves personal safety, and report the demand to the Group Compliance function as soon as practicable. The fact that a payment has been demanded under duress will not, of itself, constitute a breach of this policy; the failure to report it will.

Article 07

Third parties and due diligence

Marjan does not engage agents, brokers, consultants, or other intermediaries without first conducting due diligence proportionate to the corruption risk that the engagement presents. The depth and form of the due diligence are governed by Marjan's Third-Party Due Diligence Procedure, of which the principal elements are summarised below.

Risk-based assessment

Each prospective third party is assessed against factors that include the country of operation, the sector, the nature of the services to be performed, the identity of any politically exposed persons in the ownership chain, and the proposed level of remuneration relative to the services to be delivered.

Contractual safeguards

Contracts with third parties that present anything other than a low corruption risk include warranties of compliance with anti-corruption law, audit rights, and a right of immediate termination on credible suspicion of breach. Payment terms are structured to ensure that all amounts can be reconciled to identifiable services or deliverables.

Ongoing monitoring

Third-party relationships are reviewed periodically, with the cadence determined by the risk rating assigned at onboarding. Negative findings are escalated to the Group General Counsel and, where the threshold is met, to the Audit and Risk Committee.

Article 08

Confidential reporting channel

Non-retaliation

Marjan prohibits retaliation against any person who makes a good-faith report under this policy, whether the report ultimately proves substantiated or not. Retaliation will itself be treated as a serious breach of policy and may give rise to disciplinary action up to and including dismissal, contract termination, and referral to the competent authority.

Handling of reports

Every report received through these channels is logged, acknowledged, and assigned to an investigator independent of the area concerned. Findings are reported to the Audit and Risk Committee on a no-names basis, and to the full Board where the matter is material. Substantiated breaches are remediated, sanctioned, and, where required, referred to the competent UAE authorities.
